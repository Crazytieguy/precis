  1→# mkcert
  2→
  3→mkcert is a simple tool for making locally-trusted development certificates. It requires no configuration.
  5→```
  6→$ mkcert -install
  7→Created a new local CA 💥
  8→The local CA is now installed in the system trust store! ⚡️
  9→The local CA is now installed in the Firefox trust store (requires browser restart)! 🦊
  10→
  11→$ mkcert example.com "*.example.com" example.test localhost 127.0.0.1 ::1
  12→
  13→Created a new certificate valid for the following names 📜
  14→ - "example.com"
  15→ - "*.example.com"
  16→ - "example.test"
  17→ - "localhost"
  18→ - "127.0.0.1"
  19→ - "::1"
  20→
  21→The certificate is at "./example.com+5.pem" and the key at "./example.com+5-key.pem" ✅
  22→```
  24→<p align="center"><img width="498" alt="Chrome and Firefox screenshot" src="https://user-images.githubusercontent.com/1225294/51066373-96d4aa80-15be-11e9-91e2-f4e44a3a4458.png"></p>
  25→
  26→Using certificates from real certificate authorities (CAs) for development can be dangerous or impossible (for hosts like `example.test`, `localhost` or `127.0.0.1`), but self-signed certificates cause trust errors. Managing your own CA is the best solution, but usually involves arcane commands, specialized knowledge and manual steps.
  27→
  28→mkcert automatically creates and installs a local CA in the system root store, and generates locally-trusted certificates. mkcert does not automatically configure servers to use the certificates, though, that's up to you.
